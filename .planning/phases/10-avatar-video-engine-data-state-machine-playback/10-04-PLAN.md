  <name>Task 2: D-09 developer click-through of the full avatar sequence</name>
  <what-built>
The complete avatar video engine on the homepage: the FAQ-to-video data module, a pure state machine with a stale-event generation guard, a single persistent video element driven by a DOM-sync effect, all 15 questions across 4 category groups as plain unstyled buttons in a bottom-right fixed panel, a "Tap to play" fallback for refused autoplay, a session-scoped intro-once flag, exclusion from the print build, and a pause-on-mobile-nav-open signal.

  </what-built>

  <how-to-verify>
Run `npm run dev` and open the homepage. Work through the sequence in order — this is the D-09 click-through you committed to.

1. **Fresh-session intro (AVATAR-01).** Open a private window, or clear session storage and reload. The intro must autoplay on its own with no sound and without going fullscreen.
2. **Idle loop (AVATAR-02).** Let the intro run to the end. The idle clip must start and visibly repeat, continuously, with no stall and no black gap between loops.
3. **Answer playback (AVATAR-03).** Click any question. Its answer must start immediately, with audible sound. Confirm the video matches the question you clicked.
4. **Return to idle (AVATAR-04).** Let one answer play all the way to its end. Playback must return to the looping idle clip on its own.
5. **Mid-answer interrupt (AVATAR-05) — spend real time here.** This is the requirement most likely to be subtly wrong. Click question 1, and while it is still speaking click question 7, then question 12, then question 3, in quick succession. Each click must cut immediately to the new answer. Watch specifically for three failure signatures: audio from a previous question continuing under a new video, a flicker back to the idle clip before the new answer starts, or the widget settling on idle when it should be mid-answer. Repeat across several different pairs, including two questions in the same category and two in different categories. Then do one slow interrupt — start a question, wait until it is nearly finished, and interrupt it right at the end.
6. **Intro once per visit (AVATAR-06).** With the intro already finished, navigate to Stores, then back to Home. The idle loop must resume with no intro replay. Then close the browser entirely, reopen, and load the homepage — the intro must play again.
7. **Positioning.** On every homepage load, watch the first half-second closely. The widget must be pinned to the bottom-right corner of the viewport from the first frame. A brief appearance somewhere else, offset relative to the page content, is a real defect.
8. **All 15 reachable.** Scroll the question list. All 15 questions under 4 headings must be reachable, and the longest question — the one about a package marked delivered — must wrap across lines without being cut off.
9. **Every clip plays.** Click each of the 15 questions at least once and confirm each plays its own correct video with sound. This catches an off-by-one in the question-to-clip mapping, which nothing else in the pipeline can detect.
10. **Navigate-away silence.** Start an answer, then navigate to another page mid-playback. All audio must stop.
11. **Mobile nav pause.** Narrow the window to mobile width. Start an answer, then open the hamburger nav. Audio must stop, not merely be hidden.
12. **Print build.** Open `/print.html`. No video widget may appear anywhere on it.
  </how-to-verify>

<task type="checkpoint:human-verify" gate="blocking">
  <name>Task 3: D-10 real-iPhone verification including Low Power Mode</name>
  <what-built>
The same widget, now to be checked on real iOS hardware. The video element carries `muted`, `playsInline`, and `autoPlay` together, which is the exact combination iOS Safari requires — without `playsInline` it forces fullscreen the instant playback starts, which would destroy the floating-widget concept entirely. Every `play()` call branches on its promise, so a refusal surfaces a plain "Tap to play" control that restarts playback from inside its own click handler.
  </what-built>

  <how-to-verify>
This is the D-10 gate and it needs your physical iPhone. RESEARCH's Pitfall B is explicit that a simulator, an emulator, or desktop responsive mode cannot reproduce the Low Power Mode autoplay refusal — a pass on any of those is not a pass.

Serve the site to the phone: either run `npm run dev -- --host` and open the LAN address from the phone on the same network, or use a Vercel preview deployment.

1. **Normal mode, Safari.** Load the homepage. The intro must autoplay, silently, and stay **inline in the bottom-right corner** — it must not take over the screen. Fullscreen takeover is a hard failure.
2. **Answer with audio.** Tap a question. The answer must play with audible sound. Confirm the phone is not on silent, then confirm the audio is actually coming from the widget.
3. **Interrupt on touch.** Tap a second question mid-answer. It must cut over immediately, exactly as on desktop.
4. **Low Power Mode — the real test.** Enable Low Power Mode in Settings, Battery. Fully reload the homepage. Two outcomes are acceptable: either the intro still autoplays silently and inline, or autoplay is refused and the **"Tap to play" control appears**, and one tap starts playback. A third-party outcome — a frozen or black frame with no control and no way to start playback — is a failure and must be reported.
5. **Tap-to-play recovery.** If the fallback appeared in step 4, tap it. Playback must start and continue normally into the idle loop, and tapping a question afterwards must still play with sound.
6. **Return visit.** With the intro finished, navigate to another page and back. The intro must not replay.
7. **Layout sanity.** Confirm the widget sits inside the screen bounds with its mobile insets, is not clipped by the notch or the home indicator, and that question buttons are comfortably tappable with a thumb. Cosmetic plainness is expected and is not a defect — report only things that are unusable or clipped.
8. **Chrome or Firefox on iOS, if available.** Both use WebKit underneath, so behavior should match Safari. A divergence is worth recording even if it does not block.
  </how-to-verify>

  <resume-signal>Reply `approved` if steps 1 through 7 pass. State explicitly which Low Power Mode outcome you got — autoplay still worked, or the "Tap to play" fallback appeared and worked — since that answer determines whether the fallback is exercised in production or is only a safety net. Report any fullscreen takeover, clipping, or dead frozen frame in detail; those are the failures this gate exists to catch.</resume-signal>
